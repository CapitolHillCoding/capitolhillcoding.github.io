Privacy policy

Capitol Hill Coding collects no data at all. Here are the details.

Effective November 17, 2026

Capitol Hill Coding is a coding game for children. It is built to work entirely on your device. We don't collect, store or share any personal information, from children or anyone else, because none of it ever reaches us.

What we collect

Nothing. There are no accounts or sign-ins, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no third-party tracking or data-collecting SDKs. Apart from the store purchase described next, the app makes no network requests.

Unlocking the whole game

The first four stops are free. The rest of the game is one purchase, made behind a gate for grown-ups, through the App Store or Google Play. The store handles the payment: the app only asks the store for the price and whether the game has been bought, which is the one time it uses the network. We never see your name, your payment details or anything about your child, and nothing about what is played is sent.

On a Windows or Linux computer (from itch.io or the Microsoft Store) the whole game is bought once in that store, before it is downloaded. There the app has nothing to unlock, so it doesn't use the network at all.

Her code, progress and settings

Everything your child makes (the programs for each level, and the movies and games in the Studio), their progress, stars, and the voice and music switches are saved only in the app's private storage on your device. The app never sends them anywhere, and nobody else can read them.

Saving a game as a file

In the Studio, a movie or game can be saved as a file (its name ends in .chcpy), and a file like that can be opened. This happens only when the Save or Open button is pressed, and your device's own file chooser asks where the file goes or which file to open. The file holds only the code, as plain text: no name, no progress, and none of the pictures or sounds made for the game. Where the file goes after that (your files, a message, another device) is up to you.

Pictures and sounds for her games

In the Studio, your child can make an actor or a place for a game from a picture, taken with the camera or picked from your photos, and a sound with the microphone. The app uses the camera, the photo picker and the microphone only at that moment, when the button is pressed. The picture or sound is saved only in the app's private storage on your device, next to the game it is for. It is never uploaded, shared or looked at by anyone, and it is deleted when your child throws it away or the app is removed.

The helper, and saying things to it

In the Studio, your child can ask a small helper for code in plain words ("make the cat jump 3 times"), typed or said out loud. The helper is a tiny program inside the app, made only for this game and taught only with made-up examples. It can only write the game's own code; it can't chat or answer questions. It works out its answer on your device. What she types or says to it is never sent anywhere, and her words are not saved; only the code she chooses to tap in becomes part of her program.

The helper's box has a microphone button. The app listens only after that button is tapped, and stops when she stops talking. Where your device can turn speech into words by itself, the app asks its built-in speech recognition (part of iOS or Android, not of this app) to do the work on the device only. Where it can't, a small speech-to-words program that comes inside the app does the same job, also on the device only (it is the openly licensed Moonshine model; the app's Licenses page has its notice). Either way the app uses only the words that come back, and doesn't keep or send the sound: it is held in the device's memory while she talks and dropped as soon as it has been turned into words.

The code she writes

Programs written in the app are run by the app itself, on the device. They can't reach the internet, files, the camera, the microphone or anything else outside the game; a program can only show the pictures and play the sounds already made in the Studio. The app doesn't download code from anywhere. A game file you choose to open is run the same way, with the same limits.

The keyboard

The code editor can use your device's keyboard. What is typed stays in the app. The keyboard itself belongs to your device; if you have installed a third-party keyboard, its own privacy policy applies to it.

Voices

The characters' lines are read aloud by your device's built-in speech voice. Capitol Hill Coding hands the words to that voice on the device and doesn't record or send anything. The app never listens by itself: the microphone is used only while your child is recording a sound for a game, or after she taps the microphone to say something to the helper (see above).

Purchases

The app has no ads and nothing for sale inside it. If that ever changes, the App Store or Google Play would handle any payment, and this page would be updated first.

Permissions

The camera, the photo picker and the microphone, each asked for the first time your child makes a picture or a sound for a game. The microphone (and, on iPhone and iPad, speech recognition) is also asked for the first time she taps the helper's microphone. You can say no, and the rest of the game works the same; the helper can still be typed to. Capitol Hill Coding doesn't use location, contacts or the internet.

Children's privacy

Because we collect no personal information, there is nothing for us to review, share or delete. This is true for children under 13 (COPPA), and in every country.

Deleting your data

Deleting Capitol Hill Coding from your device removes all of its data.

Changes

If this policy ever changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
